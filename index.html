Machine Learning Researcher · Kolkata, India

I am a machine learning researcher broadly interested in representation learning and in developing new learning methods for complex and structured data. My research explores how the formulation of a learning problem, the structure imposed on data, and the mechanisms through which information is learned and transformed can shape model behaviour. I have explored these questions across multivariate time-series anomaly detection, graph representation learning, signed networks, bipartite graphs in recommender systems, feature-centric approaches to dimensionality reduction, and audio machine learning through knowledge distillation and designed acoustic descriptors.

I completed my Master's at IIITM Gwalior, where my thesis focused on multivariate time-series anomaly detection. My research investigated how complex systems could be decomposed into correlated and isolated subsystems, with their normal dynamics modelled through complementary physics-informed formulations to reconstruct normal behaviour and characterize anomalies. This approach yielded strong performance across both pointwise and range-aware anomaly-detection measures against contemporary state-of-the-art methods.

Since my Master's, my research has grown beyond the scope of my thesis through a series of independent investigations across different machine learning problems. Although these works span different domains, they reflect a continuing interest in developing learning methods around the particular structure and characteristics of the problem at hand. I am currently continuing this work while preparing for doctoral research.
